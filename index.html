Data-Efficient Hierarchical Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning via Normalizing Flows

ICRA 2025

Abstract

Hierarchical goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (H-GCRL) provides a powerful framework for tackling complex, long-horizon tasks by decomposing them into structured subgoals. However, its practical adoption is hindered by poor data efficiency and limited policy expressivity, especially in offline or data-scarce regimes. In this work, Normalizing flow-based hierarchical implicit Q-learning (NF-HIQL), a novel framework that replaces unimodal Gaussian policies with expressive normalizing flow policies at both the high- and low-levels of the hierarchy is introduced. This design enables tractable log-likelihood computation, efficient sampling, and the ability to model rich multimodal behaviors. New theoretical guarantees are derived, including explicit KL-divergence bounds for RealNVP policies and PAC-style sample efficiency results, showing that NF-HIQL preserves stability while improving generalization. Empirically, NF-HIQL is evaluted across diverse long-horizon tasks in locomotion, ball-dribbling, and multi-step manipulation from OGBench. NF-HIQL consistently outperforms prior goal-conditioned and hierarchical baselines, demonstrating superior robustness under limited data and highlighting the potential of flow-based architectures for scalable, data-efficient hierarchical reinforcement learning.

Results

Environment 1: antmaze-medium-navigate

In antmaze-medium-navigate, a quadruped MuJoCo Ant with 8 DoF must traverse a medium-sized maze—matching the classic AntMaze “medium” layout—to reach a designated goal region using only sparse, goal-reach rewards. The accompanying navigate dataset contains long-horizon trajectories, making the task a strong test of subgoal discovery and global planning while maintaining stable low-level locomotion from fixed offline data.

Environment 2: Scene-Play

Scene‑play is a manipulation tasks, designed to evaluate an agent's ability to perform object manipulation in a structured scene setting. Specifically, it involves a 6-DoF UR5e robot arm interacting with environments that incorporate multiple objects and scene elements—such as buttons, windows, and drawers—requiring the agent to execute sequential, compositional behaviors to achieve goals.

Environment 4: antsoccer-arena-navigate

AntSoccer-arena-navigate is a challenging locomotion-plus-manipulation task. In this environment, a quadrupedal Ant robot is placed in a large arena and must first navigate to a soccer ball, then dribble the ball to a designated goal position. The offline dataset contains only two kinds of demonstrations—trajectories where the Ant moves without the ball, and ones where it dribbles the ball—so solving the full task requires the agent to compose these behaviors without ever having seen the complete solution.
